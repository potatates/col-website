COL Group and Shanghai Hefu Artificial Intelligence Technology (Group) Co., Ltd. (“Hefu Group”) recently signed a strategic cooperation agreement in Beijing. The two companies will work together on robot “brain skill packs”, AI hardware and immersive experiences, the AI content ecosystem and expansion into Southeast Asia. By combining COL’s content and semantic resources with Hefu Group’s robotics technology and real-world use cases, they aim to bring AI into practical, real-world applications.

The agreement was signed by Xie Guangcai, Executive Vice President of COL Group, and Zhou Jinting, Chairman of Hefu Group.

![](2026-11-04-8l49pq/1.jpg)

Xie Guangcai said AI is moving from large language models toward world models that understand cause and effect, time and space, and that high-quality scenario data is an essential foundation for embodied intelligence. The partnership will draw on the two companies’ complementary strengths in content and semantics on one side and robot hardware and physical environments on the other. It will explore connecting semantic data with physical interaction data, building up reusable, measurable and continuously improving data resources in real-world settings to support R&D and applications.

Zhou Jinting said that while the rapid rise of AI is reshaping society, it is also creating many new opportunities and allowing individuals to unlock their creativity with AI tools. That is what inspired the two companies to build professional AI skill packs together. With embodied intelligence hardware now at a historic moment of growth, giving machines a “brain skill pack” will save users significant time and cost on secondary development, making embodied robots truly “ready to use out of the box” and delivering real value in specific scenarios.

In embodied intelligence, the two companies will combine Hefu Group’s technology with COL’s licensed IP to jointly develop robot brain skill packs for a range of scenarios, explore “AI hardware + content IP” products, and plan R&D in areas such as commercial services and government and corporate offices.

In AI hardware and immersive experiences, they will pair Hefu Group’s capabilities in embodied robots, smart home devices and AR/VR interactive devices with COL’s extensive IP library to explore an “AI hardware + content IP” model. Together they will develop products for smart cultural tourism, smart homes and offline immersive spaces, offering users new interactive experiences.

In the AI content ecosystem, they will combine COL’s XiaoYao AI with Hefu Group’s multimodal generation and virtual human technologies to jointly develop specialized AI content production skill packs for microdrama and animation. These will cover scriptwriting, storyboard previsualization, AI performance and multilingual generation, with plans to extend into editing, music and dubbing, raising production efficiency and lowering overall costs. They will also explore products and monetization around AI microdrama, virtual idols and interactive film games.

In overseas markets, COL will provide IP and its established international operations, while Hefu Group will use its local resources to handle regulatory approvals and on-the-ground operations. The two will explore forming business consortia with leading local companies to strengthen the localization of content and services, and expand into Southeast Asia, combining the content ecosystem with local operations.

Building on its licensed content and IP, COL continues to apply AI across every stage of content production. Its products include XiaoYao AI and the full-stack AI content creation platform CiYuan ShenBi, and it distributes content overseas through platforms such as FlareFlow, creating a business chain that runs from IP reserves and content production to international distribution. These content resources, AI creation tools and global distribution capabilities provide a foundation for cooperation in content production, semantic data and overseas business.

Founded in 2016, Hefu Group established the UESTC–Hefu Research Institute with the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China that same year, creating an industry–academia–research model in which “companies pose the problems, universities solve them, and industry puts the results to work.” With years of experience in AI virtual humans, intelligent robots, cloud computing and AI security, the group brings technical support to the partnership’s work on robot hardware, physical interaction and real-world applications.

“Let AI understand everyday human life, and step into the real world,” said Xie Guangcai, adding that he looks forward to working with partners to build data loops and the wider ecosystem, so that technology finds its way into concrete scenarios and products that better serve users.
